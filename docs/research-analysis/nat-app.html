Competitor Research Analysis

Nat.app: Gmail-first personal CRM for follow-up and meeting memory

Nat is narrower than Dex or Mesh, but its best ideas are extremely relevant to Wuslah: automatic follow-up detection, Gmail-native note capture, meeting prep emails, and privacy-conscious metadata use.

Company: Nat.app Website: nat.app Report date: June 1, 2026 Context: Wuslah capture and follow-up loop

Executive Readout

What Nat does

Nat connects Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Contacts, builds interaction timelines, detects people who need follow-up, and lets users take meeting notes from Gmail.

Growth signal

Public traction appears modest: Product Hunt launches, Indie Hackers reporting $500 MRR in June 2021, and small-company positioning from the founder.

Wuslah implication

Nat's note flow is a gem: do not force users into a database after a meeting. Let them capture memory in the moment and resurface it before the next interaction.

Product Workflows

Follow-up detection
  • Scans sent email conversations for threads that need follow-up.
  • Lets users send follow-ups quickly with customizable templates.
  • Opens the original Gmail thread so the follow-up happens where the relationship already lives.
Email-native notes
  • Sends meeting prep or note-taking emails around calendar events.
  • Users can reply to the email to save meeting notes.
  • Previous notes are displayed before future meetings with the same attendees.
Google-centric interaction history

Nat imports email and meeting metadata from Gmail and Google Calendar to build timelines while avoiding full email-body access.

Business context

Nat can import customer data from Stripe and Segment, making it more useful for consultants and founders than purely social networking users.

Key sources: Nat homepage, Nat follow-up, Nat note-taking, Nat privacy.

Strengths And Gaps

Strengths
  • Very clear focus on follow-ups and meeting memory.
  • Low-friction note capture through email replies.
  • Strong privacy explanation around metadata-only email use.
  • Student discount shows awareness of early-career users.
Limitations
  • Google-only integration strategy.
  • No Outlook, WhatsApp, SMS, or broad social context.
  • Annual $370 price is expensive for young professionals without the student discount.
  • Business and consultant positioning is not as warm as Wuslah's intended memory-first tone.

Wuslah Implications

Nat's biggest lesson is product ergonomics: relationship memory should be captured where the conversation happens, not after users remember to open a CRM. Wuslah can translate this into a mobile-first event and coffee-chat workflow: quick note, how-we-met context, suggested follow-up, and resurfaced talking points.

Strategic wedge: Nat helps consultants follow up from Gmail. Wuslah can help students and young professionals remember people after coffee chats, events, classes, clubs, internships, and introductions.

Product Recommendations

  1. Create a capture flow that is as easy as replying to Nat's meeting email: one tap to add a note after meeting someone.
  2. Add pre-meeting or pre-coffee-chat briefs before heavier AI features.
  3. Use metadata/privacy language early if Gmail or Calendar integrations are added.
  4. Offer a student price that is obvious, not hidden in an FAQ.
  5. Do not become Google-only. Wuslah's student audience may use text, Instagram, LinkedIn, calendar, and in-person event capture.
