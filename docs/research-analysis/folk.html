Competitor Research Analysis

folk: the team CRM benchmark for Wuslah's future enterprise layer

folk is not a direct student-memory competitor. It is a lightweight, relationship-driven CRM for teams that need LinkedIn capture, shared contacts, enrichment, AI assistance, outreach, pipelines, and collaboration. It matters for Wuslah's later team and enterprise roadmap.

Company: folk Website: folk.app Report date: June 1, 2026 Context: Wuslah future team/enterprise path

Executive Readout

What folk does

folk centralizes contacts and companies for teams, captures leads from LinkedIn, syncs email/calendar/WhatsApp, enriches data, supports notes/reminders, and runs outreach through campaigns and sequences.

Growth signal

folk reports 3,000+ teams or 4,000+ service businesses in public pages. TechCrunch reported a $4.5M raise in 2022; folk hiring material says about $9M raised with Accel and angels.

Wuslah implication

folk is a blueprint for future shared workspaces, permissions, enrichment, dashboards, and team follow-up. It is not the right tone for Wuslah's first student-first product.

Product Workflows

Relationship CRM for teams
  • Contact and company database with custom groups, views, notes, reminders, pipelines, deals, dashboards, and team permissions.
  • Shared interactions help teams know who last touched a contact and when to follow up.
  • Mobile supports contact search, notes, profile details, quick actions, and interactions.
Capture and outreach
  • folkX browser extension captures contacts from LinkedIn and the web.
  • Gmail, Outlook, Google Calendar, Google Contacts, WhatsApp, Zapier, Make, and API integrations.
  • Email campaigns, sequences, personalization, scheduling, and tracking.
AI and enrichment

AI Assistants cover follow-up detection, relationship recaps, company research notes, and trigger-based workflow automation. Enrichment providers include Clearbit, Apollo, Dropcontact, Prospeo, Datagma, and People Data Labs.

Plan packaging

Standard includes pipeline, campaigns, enrichment, AI Assistants, Magic Fields, LinkedIn extension, email/calendar/WhatsApp sync, and 5,000+ integrations. Premium adds custom objects, deals, sequences, roles, dashboards, API, and full interaction history.

Key sources: folk pricing, folkX, folk integrations, AI Assistants.

Strengths And Gaps

Strengths
  • Strong LinkedIn and browser capture workflow.
  • Modern team UX for shared relationships and outreach.
  • Built-in campaigns, sequences, dashboards, API, and enrichment.
  • Useful model for founder-led sales, partnerships, fundraising, and agency work.
Limitations
  • Too business-first and per-seat priced for Wuslah's student wedge.
  • Pipeline and outreach language can feel like sales software.
  • LinkedIn sync is not fully native; users rely on folkX or exports.
  • Mobile is newer and does not yet cover every desktop workflow.

Wuslah Implications

folk is not the right model for Wuslah's first release. It is a later-stage model for teams, shared contact pools, permissions, API access, dashboards, enrichment, and collaborative follow-up. Wuslah's immediate surface should stay personal: memory, care, context, birthdays, reminders, and warm follow-ups.

Strategic wedge: folk helps teams move relationships toward deals. Wuslah should help individuals remember people before there is any deal, pipeline, or team workflow.

Product Recommendations

  1. Use folk as a future enterprise reference for shared contacts, role-based access, dashboards, API, enrichment, and team interaction history.
  2. Avoid folk-style pipeline language in the first Wuslah UI.
  3. Build personal trust and retention before shared workspaces.
  4. When Wuslah adds teams, start with shared context and warm intros, not sales pipelines.
  5. Consider AI recaps and follow-up assistants for Premium/Pro, but phrase them as memory support rather than sales automation.
